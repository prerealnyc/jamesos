# Video Script Templates

## Short-form 3-part structure (30s version; scale proportionally)

| Time | Beat | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3s | **Hook** | Tension first: mistake, result, warning, or curiosity gap. No intro, no background. Prove the video is worth watching in line 1. Brand asset visible ≤1.5s. |
| 3–8s | **Setup/problem** | Why it matters to {audience}; remove slow setup. |
| 8–22s | **Value / retention loop** | 2–3 points, one sayable line each. The hook's question stays open. |
| 22–27s | **Payoff** | Answer the hook's open question — never earlier. |
| 27–30s | **CTA** | One lightweight ask: follow / save / comment {keyword}. |

**Prompt template:** "{hook_line}. {why_it_matters_to_audience}. Step 1: {x}. Step 2: {y}. Step 3: {z}. {payoff_answering_hook}. {one_cta}."

**Length selection:** 60–180s for substantive content if completion holds (rewarded everywhere in 2026); 10–15s for trend/replay content; Shorts are bimodal — ~13s OR ~60s, avoid the 30–45s middle.

## The But/Therefore rule (narrative connective tissue)

Replace every "and then" between beats with **BUT** (conflict) or **THEREFORE** (consequence).
**Template:** "{setup}. BUT {complication}. THEREFORE {response}. BUT {new_complication}. THEREFORE {resolution/lesson}."
Boring: "We launched, and then we got users, and then we grew." Engaging: "We launched. BUT nobody came. THEREFORE we cold-DMed 100 people a day. BUT 95 ignored us. THEREFORE we changed the pitch — and that pitch built the company."

## Pattern interrupts & open loops

- Long-form: pattern interrupt (B-roll, cut, on-screen question, angle change) every **~90 seconds** (reported +15–25% view duration). Short-form: cut every 2–5 seconds.
- Open loops: tease early, pay off late ("the third one saved me $10K… but first"). In threads/carousels: cliffhanger every 1–2 units.
- Interrupt hooks: mid-action opening, contradiction, rapid zoom, freeze-frame with text.

## YouTube long-form — 6-part retention structure

1. **Hook (0–30s):** open with the result, a bold claim, or a relatable problem. **Never introduce yourself first.**
2. **Context bridge (30–90s):** who this is for, what they'll learn, why you're credible — one line each.
3. **Body:** numbered steps, real examples, data; pattern interrupt every 90s; on-screen chapter markers.
4. **Internal CTA (mid-video):** one specific comment question at a natural pause — not "like and subscribe" walls.
5. **Outro CTA (final 60s):** reference one specific related video, not "check out my channel."
6. **End screen (final 20s):** stop speaking; let cards display; name the next video.

**Intro formula (first 30–60s), PPP:** **Preview** ("By the end you'll {outcome}") → **Proof** ("I used this to {result}") → **Payoff promise** ("and the last step is the one nobody does").

## A-roll / B-roll rules

- **60/40 rule:** ~60% talking-head, 40% B-roll for tutorials; short-form can be 100% A-roll.
- B-roll clip lengths: establishing 2–4s, detail 1.5–3s, reaction 1–2s, cutaway 2–5s. Cut before the viewer gets bored.
- Use B-roll to hide jump cuts, prove abstract claims visually, and reset attention.
- Design for sound-off: captions always; core message in on-screen text.
- Faces and genuine emotion in thumbnails/first frames lift CTR; A/B test thumbnails natively.

## Format-specific skeletons (TikTok)

- **Tutorial:** result upfront → numbered steps <60s → save CTA.
- **Storytime:** hook line → situation → conflict (BUT) → resolution (THEREFORE) → lesson.
- **Myth-busting:** "{myth}" on screen → why it's false → correct info → comment-bait CTA.
- **Before/After:** opening state → quick-cut transformation → method tease → follow CTA.
- **Reply-to-comment:** pin question on screen → detailed answer → invite next question.
