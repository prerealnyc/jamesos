# Trust & Authority Research

## 1. Edelman Trust Barometer (2026: ~34,000 respondents, 28 countries)

Trust = **Competence** (delivers on promises) × **Ethics** (does the right thing). Both axes required; competence alone reads as self-interest.

### Key numbers [measured, survey]
- "**My employer**" is the most trusted institution: **78%** (business 64%, government 53%) — durable since 2019.
- Business is the only institution rated both competent and ethical; surpassed NGOs on ethics in 2026.
- **70%** globally won't engage with people holding different values ("insularity") — cross-viewpoint engagement is now a differentiator (75% endorse CEOs doing it).
- **54%** of low-income respondents fear being left behind by generative AI.

### The messenger hierarchy (stable across editions)
Most credible spokespeople: **technical experts ≈ "a person like me" ≈ regular employees** > CEOs >> celebrities/influencers. Employees are the most trusted source for information *about a company*.

### Directives for founder-led content
1. Every founder content stream must demonstrate competence (proof, specifics, shipped work) AND ethics (values applied at a cost).
2. Deploy employees as messengers — employee-voiced content inherits the 78% employer-trust halo and outranks CEO and paid-influencer credibility.
3. Founders should take positions on issues affecting employees/customers, anchored in operational competence, not partisan identity.
4. Address AI anxiety explicitly (retraining, augmentation stance) — the top emerging fear.

## 2. Edelman–LinkedIn B2B Thought Leadership research [measured, survey of 3,500+ executives]

- ~**73%** of decision-makers trust thought leadership more than marketing materials.
- **75%+** investigated a product they hadn't considered because of thought leadership.
- **9 in 10** are more receptive to sales outreach from consistent thought-leadership producers.
- **70%** of C-suite reconsidered a supplier after reading strong TL; 25% ended/reduced relationships.
- **86%** more likely to include TL producers in RFPs.
- Effective TL = named experienced authors, distinctive format (66%), strong data (55%).
- **95% of buyers are out-of-market at any moment** — authority content is how you're remembered when they enter.
- 2025 edition: "hidden buyers" (non-obvious committee members) are reachable almost exclusively through bold, perspective-shifting TL — a specific lever for unknown/challenger brands.

## 3. Social authority signals [measured + reported]

- **Consistency streaks:** accounts posting in 20+ of 26 weeks earn ~**5× (450%) more engagement per post** than sporadic posters (Buffer, 100K+ accounts). The unit that matters is *weeks with ≥1 post*, not volume.
- **Topical focus:** TikTok's classifier needs **10–15 consistent niche posts** to reliably categorize an account; niche-focused accounts grow faster to ~100K, then broaden (documented pattern: finance-only X accounts plateau near 100K). Rule: **one niche until 10K–100K, then adjacent expansion.**
- **Employee advocacy gap:** 96% of B2B companies produce thought leadership but only 18% achieve broad employee participation — the most-trusted messenger is the least-saturated tactic.
- **Profile as proof stack:** who-you-help + outcome in bio, niche-declaring handle/banner, pinned proof post, "as seen in" PR mentions. Social proof is threshold-based: follower counts, verification and press reduce the perceived risk of following.
- **Personal > corporate:** LinkedIn personal profiles get 5–8× company-page engagement; personal content perceived ~3× more credible; a personal-profile carousel earns +63% vs the identical company-page carousel.

## 4. Operational trust rules (synthesis)

1. Founder + employee voices carry the brand; the company account is the hub (ads, hiring, credibility), not the growth engine.
2. Publish named-author, data-backed, distinct-POV thought leadership on a fixed cadence — the compounding asset for out-of-market buyers.
3. Show the work: specifics, numbers, named methods. Specificity is the cheapest authority signal.
4. Concede weaknesses first (self-deprecation, honest drawbacks) — pre-empting criticism is the cheapest authenticity signal (see Ryanair case).
5. Never fake scarcity, social proof, or credentials — detection destroys the trust multiplier on everything else.
