# Marketing Science Canon

## 1. Binet & Field / IPA — "The Long and the Short of It"

Source: IPA Effectiveness Databank (~1,000–1,400 award case studies). [measured, with selection-bias caveat]

Two advertising mechanisms run simultaneously:
- **Brand building** (long-term): emotional associations and memory structures; builds slowly, compounds, decays slowly; drives baseline sales, price insensitivity, long-run profit.
- **Sales activation** (short-term): rational persuasion of in-market buyers; sharp spikes that decay almost completely within ~6 months; does not compound.

### Directives
1. **Split effort ≈ 60% brand-building / 40% activation** (average optimum). Flex by context: online/subscription brands up to ~74% brand; B2B ~46:54.
2. **ESOV rule:** growth ≈ 0.05 × excess share of voice. ~10 points of ESOV (share of voice minus share of market) ≈ +0.5 share points/year. SOV below SOM predicts decline. Organic proxy: share of search (predicts market share with 6–12 month lead).
3. **Emotional beats rational for the long term:** very large profit growth in 43% of emotional campaigns vs 13% rational; market-share effects 47% vs 16%. Rational wins only in short windows near purchase.
4. **Fame campaigns** (content that gets talked about) outperform everything: ~34% profit improvement, up to 4× efficiency; renew creative every 2–3 years.
5. **Target the whole category, not loyalists:** penetration campaigns produce very large effects 22% of cases vs 7% loyalty-focused; new-customer campaigns ~3× as effective as loyalty campaigns.
6. **Creative quality multiplier:** creatively awarded campaigns are ~11× more efficient at converting ESOV into share growth.
7. **Measure over ≥12 months**; emotional campaigns only pull ahead after ~6 months.

### Content application
- Classify every asset as brand (broad-reach, emotional, distinctive, no CTA required) or activation (targeted, offer/CTA); enforce ~60/40 over rolling quarters.
- Broad reach beats tight targeting even in social — herd/social-proof effects require non-buyers to see it too.
- Video/AV formats are the strongest brand-building carriers.

Limits: databank = self-selected award entries; 60/40 is an average, not a law; ESOV harder to compute organically.

## 2. Ehrenberg-Bass / Byron Sharp — "How Brands Grow"

Source: decades of cross-category panel data. [measured]

### The empirical laws
- **Double Jeopardy:** small brands have fewer buyers who are also slightly less loyal. Loyalty is a function of size; you cannot build it independently of penetration.
- **Growth = penetration** (more buyers), not frequency/loyalty.
- **Pareto is ~60/20:** top 20% of buyers deliver only ~50–60% of volume; roughly half of sales come from light buyers (median Coca-Cola buyer: 1–2 Cokes/year). Light and non-buyers are the growth audience.
- **Buyer moderation:** heavy buyers regress to the mean; last year's heavy buyers systematically disappoint.
- **Duplication of purchase:** you share customers with all competitors in proportion to their size; no partitioned loyal segments.
- **Attitudes follow behavior:** users say nice things because they're users; attitudinal tracking lags.

### The two growth assets
- **Mental availability** — probability of being thought of in buying situations. Built via **Category Entry Points (CEPs)**: the occasions, needs, moods, locations, co-purchases through which buyers enter the category. Target: link the brand to **6–10 key CEPs**. Content form: "when {cep} happens, think {brand}."
- **Physical availability** — ease of finding/buying everywhere buying situations occur. Digital form: findability in search, social search, marketplaces, AI assistants.

### Distinctive brand assets (Romaniuk)
Score every non-name element (color, logo, character, sonic, tagline) on **Fame** (% who link it to the brand) × **Uniqueness** (% of links that go only to you):
- High/High → use everywhere, always, prominently.
- High Fame/Low Unique → sharpen or pair (it's feeding competitors).
- Low Fame/High Unique → invest and repeat consistently.
- Low/Low → redesign or kill.
Maintain a core of **3–5 assets**; retest annually.

### Directives
1. Reach all category buyers continuously; always-on beats bursts (advertising works mostly by refreshing decaying memories).
2. Optimize for light/non-buyers: assume the viewer barely knows you; never require insider knowledge.
3. Brand early and often: distinctive assets in the first seconds of every asset and every choice point (thumbnails, handles, covers).
4. Map the category's CEPs; create content pairing the brand with underexploited ones.
5. Be distinctive over differentiated: consistent famous cues beat uniqueness claims.
6. Don't build growth strategy on loyalty/retention messaging; defection is largely structural.
7. Broad reach > engagement-rate optimization; engagement indexes heavy users — the wrong growth audience.

Limits: FMCG-heaviest evidence; contested for subscriptions/luxury/B2B; conflicts with Kantar on differentiation (resolution: treat *distinctiveness* as non-negotiable, *meaningful difference* as a pricing/margin lever).

## 3. Kantar BrandZ — Meaningful · Different · Salient

Source: ~4.5M consumer interviews, 21K brands; MASB-certified link to financial outcomes. [measured, proprietary weighting]

- **Meaningful** = meets functional + emotional needs. **Different** = offers what others don't, sets trends. **Salient** = comes to mind quickly for category needs.
- Predictive indices: Demand Power (volume equity commands), Pricing Power (sustainable premium), Future Power (12-month growth probability).

### Key numbers
- Brand-driven consumers pay **+37%** for Meaningfully Different brands; even price-driven pay +14%.
- Only ~25% of growing brands grew via Salience alone; ~75% via Meaningful Difference.
- Converting strong equity into salience yields ~3× the share gain of salience built on weak equity.
- Difference is the biggest driver of growth and downturn resilience.

### Directives
1. Diagnose every content stream on all three: need met (Meaningful)? unlike alternatives (Different)? distinctive/frequent enough (Salient)?
2. Never optimize salience alone — it multiplies equity, it isn't equity.
3. Articulate Difference explicitly (unique POV, proprietary method, trend-setting) — this defends margin.
4. Pricing power is a brand-content KPI: if prices can't rise without volume collapse, Meaningful Difference is weak regardless of engagement.

## 4. Creative Quality & Attention Research

### Creative's share of sales impact [measured]
- Nielsen Catalina (2017, ~500 CPG campaigns): **creative = 49% of ad-driven sales lift**; media factors 36% (reach 22, targeting 9, recency 5); brand 15%.
- NCSolutions update (2023, ~450 campaigns): creative **still 49%**; brand rose to 21%; reach fell to 14%; targeting 11%.
- Marketers systematically underestimate creative and overestimate targeting (industry ranks targeting first; data ranks it ~4th).
- **Rule: treat creative quality as ~50% of outcome variance.** Creative testing/iteration earns more ROI than audience micro-targeting.

### Attention thresholds [measured]
- **2.5-second attention-memory threshold** (Amplified Intelligence): active attention below ~2.5s does not reliably encode brand memory; >80% of ad exposures fail to clear it.
- **1.5-second formula** (VCCP/Nelson-Field 2025): brand assets must land within the first 1.5 seconds in low-attention feeds.
- **Lumen/Teads:** ~8+ attentive seconds to move purchase intent, ~9+ for consideration; attention predicts outcomes better than viewability at every funnel stage. Plan on attentive seconds, not impressions.

### Directives
1. Distinctive brand asset + core message inside the first 1.5–2.5 seconds of any video; design for sound-off.
2. Most impressions deliver <2.5s — build branding-first creative that works at that duration; reserve persuasion arcs for high-attention placements (YouTube long-form, podcasts, CTV).
3. Invest disproportionately in creative pre-testing/iteration (~half of sales impact) over targeting refinement (~10%).
